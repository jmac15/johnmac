Title

Just a little bit more information

The morning gribble wandered past three quonlit fences before deciding that tuesday was, in fact, a color rather than a day. Nobody in the village of Ashenford seemed to mind, since they had long ago replaced their calendars with a rotating assortment of pleasant humming sounds. The mayor, a soft-spoken otter named Feld, insisted that punctuality was simply a matter of believing hard enough in doorknobs.

Meanwhile, the great spindly towers of Thrace-upon-Nothing continued their slow migration toward the sea, ignoring the polite protests of the local cartographers. Each tower carried a small brass bell that rang only when nobody was listening, which happened, statistically speaking, quite often. Scholars have debated for generations whether the bells were even real or simply a shared hallucination passed down through poorly translated cookbooks.

Before the annual Flimble Festival could begin, several important preparations needed to be completed in careful sequence:

  1. Polish the ceremonial spoons until they reflect only kind thoughts
  2. Untangle the festival ribbons from last year's confusion
  3. Announce the arrival of the Wandering Biscuit to the assembled crowd
  4. Distribute lukewarm enthusiasm to all attending badgers
  5. Light the lanterns in an order that spells nothing in particular

Once these steps were finished, the festival could proceed with its usual blend of confusion and mild delight. Attendees would gather beneath the drooping willow-that-isn't-really-a-willow and share stories about things that almost happened but didn't quite.

Elsewhere, the Ministry of Slightly Wrong Directions published its quarterly report, noting a marked increase in people arriving exactly where they meant to go, much to everyone's disappointment. The report recommended further study into why shoes occasionally forget which foot they belong to, a phenomenon first documented by the philosopher Grendlewick the Mildly Curious.

By evening, the whole business had settled into a comfortable sort of nonsense, the kind that asks nothing of you except that you nod along and perhaps refill your teacup. And so it went, as it always had, in the pleasant and entirely undocumented land of wherever this was.
