Digital literacies

Digital literacy and why it matters - 2 minutes 54 seconds

Jisc define digital literacies as “the capabilities which fit someone for living, learning and working in a digital society” Jisc.

Digital literacies have become increasingly important in a world where technology and digital platforms have become virtually ubiquitous. This concept has become especially important in education, with institutions preparing students for employment and engagement in wider culture and society.

While digital literacies would include skills related to the use of technology, it goes far beyond this. Digital literacies are driven by context and include the way we engage with digital platforms and how that in turn shapes our identity.

Beetham and Sharpe developed a framework examining the way we develop digital literacies (Jisc 2014). This framework shows how an individual’s digital literacy can develop over time. The steps are listed below in ascending order:

  • Functional access – “I have…”
  • Skills development – “I can…”
  • Situated practices – “I do…”
  • Identity development – “I am…”

The importance of digital literacies go beyond education; they will influence how we engage with the world culturally and economically. The implications for those lacking relevant digital literacies are described by Wheeler:

“Those who are not digitally literate may find themselves on the periphery of the connected society. They will become a part of what is now known as the digital divide.”

Wheeler (2015: 166)
